Three keys

The key that spends can never raise its own limit

Three keys, three frequencies, three sets of things each may authorise. The split is not a policy document: the resolver’s role checks enforce it, and the proof is a transaction that fails.

Authority flows downward only. If a change would let the hot tier widen its own mandate, rotate a key, or move a payout address, the change is wrong.

Three wallet vendors is not three ways to do one thing. Each sits where it is actually best, and together they are one hierarchy. What matters in the table below is the last column, because that is the column the chain enforces.

TierWho holds itHow often it signsMay authorise
Cold A separate key. Custody is not cold today — see below Once per lifecycle Hot-key rotation, payout address change, price-ceiling raise
Warm Privy server wallet, owned by a 1-of-2 ops quorum Occasionally Issuing a mandate, raising a cap (needs a separate 2-of-2 board quorum), adding an agent
Hot Circle / Arc Agent Stack — the buyer agent’s spending wallet Every query Nothing. It spends within the mandate, signs offchain, and never submits a transaction

Every row of that table has been corrected at least once during this build, and the full record of what was withdrawn and why is in CORRECTIONS.md. That file is linked here on purpose. A judge who finds a retraction we hid stops believing the rest of the page.

The proof is a transaction that fails

Two distinct keys exist on the seller side. The operator key 0x0Adca6e1…bf5F2 holds the root roles on liquidity.turnstile.eth. The hot key 0x16244874…186367 holds roles scoped to exactly two records — agent-endpoint[mcp] and turnstile:price — and to nothing else. PermissionedResolver scopes roles by resource(node, part), where part identifies a record type. That is what makes a role scopeable to one text key on one name rather than to the whole name.

So the seller can reprice all day from a key sitting in a hot wallet, and that same key cannot move where the money goes. Asking it to try produces this:

$ cast call $R "setAddr(bytes32,address)" $N 0x...dEaD --from $TURNSTILE_HOT_KEY Error: execution reverted: EACUnauthorizedAccountRoles( 87140393317148622081856579921496229094570723339484417348903196786160147430199, 1, 0x16244874881c60881CEA827fe856Ea91d3186367 )

Live Sepolia, 2026-09-07. 1 is ROLE_SET_ADDR; the resource is the name-level one, which is where onlyPartRoles reverts once no part-scoped grant matched.

Claim

The hot key cannot move the payout address, cannot raise its own price ceiling, and cannot grant itself the roles to do either.

Proof
Three separate reverts on live Sepolia: EACUnauthorizedAccountRoles on setAddr, the same on setText("turnstile:price-ceiling"), and EACCannotGrantRoles on all three self-grant routes.
Test
testFork_hotKeyCannotChangeThePayoutOnChain in contracts/test/fork/SepoliaOffer.t.sol — a fork test against live Sepolia, not a mock.
And still works
testFork_hotKeyCanStillReprice and testFork_hotKeyIsStillScopedToTwoRecords, so the restriction is scoped rather than a dead key.
Where
docs/ens-offer-records.md → Live proof: what the hot key can and cannot do

A stolen hot key costs this seller a wrong price for as long as it takes the operator key to rotate it. It cannot cost them a single payment. That is a much smaller claim than “our keys are secure”, and unlike that claim it is one you can falsify in an afternoon.

The same shape on the buyer’s side

The seller half is enforced by a resolver; the buyer half is enforced by a Privy policy and by the absence of gas. The buyer’s organisation wallet — a Privy server wallet at 0x3De96375140717193f52c220Df5Ec460971cbE84 — funds the agent by calling depositFor(amount, agent): the org pays the deposit’s gas, and the resulting Gateway balance belongs to the agent.

Two quorums own the two halves, and the difference between them is the control. A 1-of-2 operations quorum owns the wallet, so funding the agent takes one signature. A separate 2-of-2 board quorum owns the mandate policy, so raising the cap takes two — and Privy returns 401 when only one is offered. Verified live rather than described: npm run privy:mandate performs the refusal and then the two-signature success. The transcript and two on-chain transactions are in docs/privy-mandate.md. The agent itself, meanwhile, holds no gas at all, so it cannot deposit, withdraw or widen its allowance even if it wanted to — each of those is a transaction, and it cannot submit one.

What we are not claiming

This is the first of the two places this project is weakest, and it is here at full size rather than in a footnote.

Not claimed

Custody is not cold. The operator key is the same address as the deployer, and its private key sits in .env on a working laptop.

Why
addresses.turnstile.sepolia.json records coldKey and deployer as the same address, 0x0Adca6e14bA956201D221feC767e4f24194bf5F2, and that key is DEPLOYER_PRIVATE_KEY, loaded by every deploy script. It is not offline in any sense.
Consequence
What the tiers separate is authority, not custody. Do not read the word “cold” in the table above as cold storage, hardware, or an air gap. Compromise that laptop and you have the operator key.
Scope
The authority split is unaffected and is the claim worth making: a separate key holds the only roles that can move the payout address, and the chain enforces it — that is the revert above, and it is real. Making custody genuinely cold means generating that key on an offline machine, keeping it out of .env, and signing the rare cold-tier transactions air-gapped. That is not done.
History
This row previously named a Ledger device, and then claimed “held offline”. Both were withdrawn, dated, in CORRECTIONS.md. We got this wrong twice; saying so is cheaper than being found out once.

One more honest edge, smaller than the above. The ceiling binds by convention at read time, not mechanically at write time: the resolver enforces who may write which key, and comparing turnstile:price against turnstile:price-ceiling is the buyer’s agent’s job — two text() calls. Making it bind on chain means routing the hot key’s writes through a gatekeeper contract that holds the grant and rejects price > ceiling. That is a clean follow-up and deliberately not done here, because it changes who holds the authorization.
