Keep the method

Sell the answer, keep the method

Page 01’s third failure: publishing the analyst reveals the analyst, so the sellers with the most to offer have the most reason to stay off the market. A confidential workflow answers both halves — the buyer cannot reconstruct the method, and cannot be defrauded either.

A verdict you have to take on trust is worth nothing, and a method you have to publish to prove is worth nothing once published. Those pull in opposite directions, which is why most “sell your model” designs quietly pick one. An attested enclave splits the difference at exactly the right seam: the shape of the judgement is public, and the calibration is not.

WhatHow it gets inConfidential?
in The seller’s calibration — the tuned thresholds Vault DON secret Yes
in The evidence bundle — 9,218 bytes of subgraph history and live depth Confidential HTTP, gated on a Vault-held bearer token Yes
in The scorer — seller/analyst/scoring.ts Compiled into the workflow binary No, deliberately
out Rating, confidence, two 7-bit signal masks, keccak256(evidence) writeReport to chain Public

That third row is the one it would be easy to overclaim, so: the workflow binary is not confidential. The Workflow DON supplies it to the enclave, and it is MIT and readable in this repo. What the enclave protects is the data the binary computes over. Which is the right way round. A public scorer is what makes a verdict auditable rather than an oracle: you can see which seven signals, which are structural, and how one structural failure forces AVOID.

The calibration is the edge, and it is what live data buys. activeHourFailShare is 0.2 rather than 0.1 only because a real TRUMP/WETH run said so — three traded hours out of twenty-four, $1.54 of volume between them, coming back as merely “bursty” at the looser threshold. A constant compiled into the binary would have been published; a Calibration argument can arrive from a Vault.

Where the sealed calibration changes the answer

This is the demonstration that the confidentiality is load-bearing rather than decorative. Run the same pool through the public scorer and through the sealed one and you get two different verdicts:

evidence: 9218 bytes for Uniswap v3 USDC/WETH 0.05%, keccak256 0x60caa3841046ee545a5a91881ba92e5ce201a49048f3d3ec08ad5369c2bd02c2 verdict: CAUTION at 75% confidence; warning on depth-vs-tvl, slippage-curve public calibration says ACCEPTABLE at 75%, the sealed one says CAUTION at 75% -- this is the difference the Vault secret bought

From cre workflow simulate, 2026-09-07. Anyone can run the public scorer on this pool and get ACCEPTABLE. The premium verdict is CAUTION, and the difference is the thing being sold.

Claim

The verdict that settled on Sepolia is byte-identical to the one the enclave produced.

Proof
Same evidence hash 0x60caa384…02c2 over the same 9,218 bytes, same rating (CAUTION), same confidence (7500 bp), same fail and warn masks. Read back off chain after the write, not asserted.
Transaction
0x8ee33bb3…e909b8 — success, block 11654749, gas 170327.
Why it holds
assess() is a total function of its arguments, so the same evidence and the same calibration give the same verdict inside a TEE and outside one. The rehearsal exercises that rather than asserting it.
Rerun it
npm run settle, and docs/cre-confidential-workflow.md for the full transcript.

What a buyer gets, then, is a verdict plus keccak256 of the exact evidence bundle it was computed over. They can check that the chain commits to the bundle they were handed, and they still cannot reproduce the judgement, because the thresholds that produced it never left the Vault. That is the whole trade.

What we are not claiming

This is the second of the two places this project is weakest, and like the first it is on the page at full size. A judge will check the forwarder; better that they find this paragraph first.

Not claimed

That settled verdict is not DON-attested. Our own key delivered it, and the production consumer would reject it.

Why
Two separate gates, and being exact about them matters more than a green tick. cre workflow deploy needs deployment access — cre account access answered “Deployment access is not yet enabled for your organization” on 2026-09-07 — and Confidential Workflows is separately in private beta. Neither gates simulate, which is why everything above ran.
Consequence
The transaction linked above went to the rehearsal consumer 0xEE72d3d0E4b090eBB8Db6abc26547bcd1fc9C5F2, whose forwarder is the seller’s own key. Do not cite it as attested. The enclave works and the settlement works; the link between them is signed by us, not by the DON.
Checked in code
AttestationStrength has four values rather than being a boolean, and run against the rehearsal consumer today it reports self-delivered — in a sentence a buyer can act on. Collapsing those four into verified: true is the one bug that would make the premium tier worthless.
Scope
The production consumer 0xfE95CD0f…eEeA exists, is Etherscan-verified, and is gated on the real CRE Forwarder 0xF8344CFd…4482. Nothing has settled into it, and it would refuse the write above. That is the point of deploying it: the honest claim has an address.
AttestationStrengthWhat it means

forwarded-ungatedThe CRE Forwarder, but the consumer would accept any workflow this owner deploys

A related piece of the same discipline: an unjudged pool reads INSUFFICIENT_DATA, never ACCEPTABLE — on chain too, where verdictOf() reverts rather than returning a zero struct, because a zeroed Verdict decodes as ACCEPTABLE at 0% confidence. Silence must not read as approval, in a struct any more than in a document.
